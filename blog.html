1. How to Make a Professional Resume for Freshers

A fresher resume introduces you to an employer when you have limited or no full-time work experience. Focus on education, practical abilities, projects and your readiness to learn. Keep the document clear, honest and relevant to the job.

Recommended sections

  1. Contact details: Name, professional email, phone number and city.
  2. Profile or objective: Two or three lines about the role you seek and your relevant strengths.
  3. Education: Latest qualification first, with institution, board or university and year.
  4. Skills: Job-related skills you can demonstrate.
  5. Projects or training: Explain what you did and what you learned.
  6. Certificates and achievements: Include genuine, relevant items.
Example: “Recent commerce graduate with practical knowledge of MS Excel and basic accounting, seeking an entry-level accounts assistant role.” Edit this to match your real background.

One page is often enough for a fresher. Proofread spelling, dates, phone number and email before applying.

2. Resume Format for 10th and 12th Pass Students

Students can create a useful resume without formal employment. Include contact details, education, computer knowledge, languages, school projects, volunteering, sports or activities that show responsibility.

Suggested structure

  • Name and reliable contact details.
  • A short objective for the entry-level role or apprenticeship you want.
  • Class 10 and 12 school, board, passing year and marks or grade if relevant.
  • Computer skills and languages you genuinely know.
  • Projects, responsibilities, volunteering and certificates, if applicable.
Sample objective: “Motivated Class 12 graduate seeking an entry-level office assistant opportunity. Comfortable with basic computer tasks and eager to learn workplace processes.”

Do not invent experience or certificates. If you have no work history, highlight genuine school projects, coursework and practical skills.

3. Best Resume Format for Experienced Candidates

A reverse-chronological resume is often effective for experienced candidates: show your current or most recent role first, followed by earlier roles. Make your responsibilities, relevant achievements and career progression easy to scan.

  1. Contact information and concise professional summary.
  2. Relevant skills matching the target role.
  3. Work experience in reverse chronological order.
  4. Education and relevant certifications.
  5. Selected projects or achievements.

For each job, include title, employer, location and dates. Use concise bullet points and explain contributions. Add measurable outcomes only when they are accurate and verifiable. One or two pages may be suitable depending on relevant experience; do not add filler.

4. How to Write a Career Objective in a Resume

A career objective is a short statement near the top of a resume. It can be useful for students, freshers, career changers or applicants for a defined entry-level role. Explain the opportunity you seek and the relevant strengths you bring.

Simple formula

Target role + relevant qualification or strength + contribution you hope to make.

  • Fresher: “Recent graduate with a foundation in business communication and Excel, seeking an entry-level operations role.”
  • Office assistant: “Organised candidate with basic computer and document-handling skills, seeking an office assistant position.”
  • Career change: “Customer-service professional moving into sales coordination, bringing client communication and record-keeping experience.”

Customise examples honestly. Avoid vague statements such as “I want a challenging job in a reputed company.” Keep the objective to two or three lines.

5. Top Resume Skills for Job Seekers

The right skills depend on the job. Read the job description and select skills you can support with examples from study, projects, training or work.

Transferable skills

Written communication, verbal communication, teamwork, time management, problem solving, attention to detail, adaptability, organisation, customer service, planning and prioritisation.

Practical and digital skills

MS Word, MS Excel, PowerPoint, email tools, data entry, basic bookkeeping, presentation skills, online research, billing or inventory software, and role-specific tools.

Do not list every skill just to fill space. Choose around six to ten relevant skills and describe your level honestly. Name software only if you have actually used it.

6. How to Write Education Details in a Resume

List your most recent qualification first. Include qualification, school or college, board or university, location and completion year. Add marks, percentage, CGPA or grade when relevant or requested.

Example format:
Diploma in [Subject]
[Institute Name], [City] | [Year of completion]
Relevant coursework: [Course, if useful]

If you are still studying, write “Expected completion: [Month, Year]”. List short courses or certificates separately from formal education. Check institution names and dates, and never claim a qualification you have not earned.

7. How to Write Work Experience in a Resume

Explain what you were responsible for and what you contributed. Include employer, job title, location and dates. Relevant internships, freelance work, apprenticeships and volunteering can also be included in clearly labelled sections.

Make bullet points useful

  • Start with an action verb: prepared, assisted, maintained, coordinated or resolved.
  • Describe the task, tool or responsibility.
  • Add a result or context when it is accurate.

Basic: “Handled customer calls.”
More informative: “Responded to customer enquiries, recorded requests and referred complex issues to the supervisor.”

If you have no formal experience, do not create a fake job. Highlight relevant projects, coursework and training instead.

8. 10 Common Resume Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Spelling and grammar errors — proofread carefully.
  2. Using one resume for every job — tailor relevant details.
  3. Incorrect contact details — check phone and email.
  4. Too much information — remove irrelevant details.
  5. Unsupported claims — never exaggerate skills or experience.
  6. Complicated formatting — use readable fonts and spacing.
  7. Long paragraphs — prefer concise bullet points.
  8. Unnecessary personal details — include only appropriate information.
  9. Missing dates or job titles — make your timeline clear.
  10. Unclear file name — use a professional name such as Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.

Open the final PDF on a phone or computer. Check that text is readable and no content is cut off.

9. Best Resume Format for Private Jobs in India

Different private-sector roles need different evidence. Adapt your resume to the vacancy rather than using one fixed version for every industry.

  • Retail/customer service: Customer interaction, product knowledge, billing or point-of-sale experience.
  • Office/admin: Document handling, email, scheduling, Excel and record keeping.
  • Accounts: Relevant education, Excel, bookkeeping or accounting software you know.
  • Sales: Communication, product demonstrations, follow-up and verified results.
  • Technical roles: Tools, projects and practical work relevant to the vacancy.

Use job-advertisement keywords naturally when they accurately describe your abilities. Avoid adding sensitive personal information unless it is specifically required and appropriate.

10. How to Create a Resume and Download It as a PDF

PDF is commonly used for job applications because it helps preserve layout across devices. Complete your content and proofread it before saving.

  1. Choose a clean layout suitable for your experience.
  2. Enter contact details and a short profile or objective.
  3. Add education, relevant skills, experience and projects.
  4. Review the preview and correct spelling, dates and spacing.
  5. Use the PDF download or print option. In the print dialog, choose “Save as PDF” where available.
  6. Open the saved PDF and check every page before sending it.
Privacy reminder: Enter only the personal information needed for your resume. Use a device you trust and review the privacy information of any online service before entering personal details.
